No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Miami Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that most of them develop out of sight. Minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your unit has left.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Miami Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank gets a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water needs regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a household of five cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your equipment and water, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

The payoff shows up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat more efficiently, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Miami Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your morning rush, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM to the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes around Miami run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Miami Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is climbing, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
